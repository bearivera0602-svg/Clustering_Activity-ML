This activity applies the K-Means clustering algorithm to the Mall Customers dataset to identify groups of customers with similar characteristics based on age, annual income, and spending score. The activity includes data preprocessing, feature scaling, initial clustering with K = 3, evaluation of different cluster counts using the Elbow Method and Silhouette Score, final cluster visualization, and interpretation of the resulting customer segments.

Dataset source: Kaggle — [Customer Segmentation Tutorial in Python](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/vjchoudhary7/customer-segmentation-tutorial-in-python).

The dataset contains 200 mall customer records, including customer ID, gender, age, annual income, and spending score. This project uses age, annual income, and spending score as the numerical features for K-Means clustering.

# OUTLIER ASSESSMENT USING THE IQR METHOD

numerical_cols = [
    "Age",
    "Annual Income (k$)",
    "Spending Score (1-100)"
]

outlier_summary = []

for col in numerical_cols:
    Q1 = df[col].quantile(0.25)
    Q3 = df[col].quantile(0.75)
    IQR = Q3 - Q1

    lower_bound = Q1 - 1.5 * IQR
    upper_bound = Q3 + 1.5 * IQR

    outlier_count = (
        (df[col] < lower_bound) | (df[col] > upper_bound)
    ).sum()

    outlier_summary.append({
        "Feature": col,
        "Lower Bound": lower_bound,
        "Upper Bound": upper_bound,
        "Outlier Count": outlier_count
    })

outlier_df = pd.DataFrame(outlier_summary)

print("Outlier Assessment Using the IQR Method:")
display(outlier_df)

Outlier Assessment

The Interquartile Range (IQR) method was used to identify potential outliers in the numerical features. The results showed no detected outliers in Age or Spending Score, while Annual Income had two potential outliers. These observations were flagged because their annual income values fell outside the IQR-based lower and upper bounds. However, potential outliers are not automatically errors. Since these values may represent actual customers, they will be retained unless further investigation shows that they are invalid. This approach preserves potentially meaningful customer information for the K-Means clustering analysis.

# Test K values from 2 to 10
k_values = range(2, 11)

inertias = []
silhouette_scores = []

for k in k_values:
    model = KMeans(
        n_clusters=k,
        random_state=42,
        n_init=10
    )

    labels = model.fit_predict(X_scaled)

    # Elbow method: within-cluster sum of squares
    inertias.append(model.inertia_)

    # Silhouette score: cluster separation and cohesion
    silhouette_scores.append(
        silhouette_score(X_scaled, labels)
    )

# Display the results
results = pd.DataFrame({
    "K": list(k_values),
    "Inertia (SSE)": inertias,
    "Silhouette Score": silhouette_scores
})

display(results.round(4))

# Elbow graph
plt.figure(figsize=(8, 5))
plt.plot(k_values, inertias, marker="o")
plt.title("Elbow Method for Mall Customers")
plt.xlabel("Number of Clusters (K)")
plt.ylabel("Inertia (SSE)")
plt.xticks(list(k_values))
plt.grid(True, alpha=0.3)
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

Bakit K = 6?¶

  • Elbow Method: Bumababa ang inertia habang tumataas ang K, pero paliit nang paliit ang improvement. Ang graph ang gagamitin para makita ang point kung saan hindi na ganoon kalaki ang dagdag na improvement.
  • Silhouette Score: Ang (K=6) ang may pinakamataas na score sa mga sinubukan, na 0.4284. Ipinapahiwatig nito na mas mahusay ang separation at cohesion ng clusters kumpara sa ibang tested values.
  • Overall decision: Pinili ang (K=6) dahil ito ang may pinakamataas na Silhouette Score sa tested range, habang isinasaalang-alang din ang Elbow graph. Ang score na 0.4284 ay nagpapahiwatig ng moderate clustering structure, hindi perpektong paghihiwalay ng mga grupo.

from sklearn.cluster import KMeans

final_kmeans = KMeans(
    n_clusters=6,
    random_state=42,
    n_init=10
)

final_labels = final_kmeans.fit_predict(X_scaled)

# Add cluster assignments
df_final = df_clean.copy()

    df_final.groupby("Cluster")

        Customer_Count=("Cluster", "size"),

        Average_Income=("Annual Income (k$)", "mean"),
        Average_Spending=("Spending Score (1-100)", "mean")

    cluster_summary["Customer_Count"] / len(df_final) * 100

Final Cluster Analysis (K = 6)¶

The final K-Means model divided the 200 mall customers into six clusters using Age, Annual Income, and Spending Score. The cluster profiles were examined using the average values of these features and the percentage of customers in each cluster.

Cluster 0 – Older Customers with Moderate Spending¶

This cluster contains 45 customers (22.5%). The average age is 56.33 years, annual income is 54.27 (in thousands of dollars), and spending score is 49.07. These customers are relatively older, with moderate income and spending behavior. Potential marketing strategy: Offer convenient shopping experiences, loyalty rewards, and promotions suited to their purchasing preferences.

Cluster 1 – Younger Customers with Moderate Spending¶

This cluster contains 39 customers (19.5%). The average age is 26.79 years, annual income is 57.10 (in thousands of dollars), and spending score is 48.13. These customers are relatively young, with moderate income and spending scores. Potential marketing strategy: Use targeted promotions, seasonal offers, and loyalty programs to encourage repeat visits.

Cluster 2 – High-Income, Low-Spending Customers¶

This cluster contains 33 customers (16.5%). The average age is 41.94 years, annual income is 88.94 (in thousands of dollars), and spending score is 16.97. They have the highest average income among the six clusters but the lowest average spending score. Potential marketing strategy: Test personalized offers, premium product recommendations, and customer engagement campaigns to understand what might encourage more spending.

Cluster 3 – High-Income, High-Spending Customers¶

This cluster contains 39 customers (19.5%). The average age is 32.69 years, annual income is 86.54 (in thousands of dollars), and spending score is 82.13. These customers combine high income with high spending scores, making them a potentially valuable segment for targeted campaigns. Potential marketing strategy: Consider VIP rewards, exclusive promotions, and early access to selected products or events.

Cluster 4 – Younger Customers with Low Income and High Spending¶

This cluster contains 23 customers (11.5%). The average age is 25.00 years, annual income is 25.26 (in thousands of dollars), and spending score is 77.61. They have relatively low income but high spending scores. Potential marketing strategy: Offer affordable trend-focused products, student-friendly promotions, and budget-conscious bundles.

Cluster 5 – Lower-Income Customers with Low Spending¶

This cluster contains 21 customers (10.5%). The average age is 45.52 years, annual income is 26.29 (in thousands of dollars), and spending score is 19.38. These customers have relatively low income and low spending scores. Potential marketing strategy: Test value deals, practical product bundles, and discounts while monitoring customer response.

Comparison and Business Applications¶

The six clusters reveal differences in customer age, annual income, and spending behavior. In particular, Cluster 3 represents customers with both high average income and high spending scores, while Cluster 2 represents customers with high income but low spending scores. Cluster 4 shows that high spending scores do not necessarily correspond to high annual income. A mall could use these segments to develop more tailored promotions, loyalty programs, product recommendations, and customer engagement strategies. These suggestions are potential applications of the observed patterns; actual campaign effectiveness would need to be tested using real business outcomes.

Conclusion¶

This activity used the K-Means clustering algorithm to segment 200 mall customers based on three numerical features: Age, Annual Income, and Spending Score. The features were standardized using StandardScaler before clustering to ensure that differences in measurement scales would not disproportionately influence the results. The Elbow Method and Silhouette Score were used to evaluate different numbers of clusters, from K = 2 to K = 10. Among the tested values, K = 6 achieved the highest Silhouette Score of 0.4284. Therefore, six clusters were selected for the final model, considering both the inertia trend and the silhouette results. The final model identified six customer segments with different age, income, and spending profiles. For example, Cluster 3 contained customers with high average annual income and high spending scores, while Cluster 2 contained customers with high average income but low spending scores. These differences demonstrate how clustering can reveal customer patterns that may help businesses develop more targeted marketing strategies. The PCA visualization provided a two-dimensional representation of the clusters based on the standardized features. The first two principal components explained approximately 77.57% of the total variance, allowing the cluster structure to be visualized while retaining much of the information in the original features. Overall, K-Means clustering can support customer segmentation and data-driven marketing decisions. The results provide a useful starting point for understanding customer behavior and designing targeted promotions, loyalty programs, and customer engagement strategies.

Limitations¶

  1. Moderate cluster separation: The highest Silhouette Score was 0.4284, indicating moderate separation rather than clearly distinct clusters. Some customers may have similar characteristics across different clusters.
  2. Dependence on feature selection: The results were based only on Age, Annual Income, and Spending Score. Including other relevant information, such as purchase frequency or transaction history, could produce different customer segments.
  3. Sensitivity to initialization and outliers: K-Means can produce different results depending on centroid initialization and may be influenced by extreme values. Two potential outliers were detected in Annual Income using the IQR method and were retained because they may represent valid customers.
  4. Dataset limitations: The dataset contains 200 customer records and a limited set of attributes. The resulting segments may not represent all mall customers or customers from other locations.
  5. Clusters do not explain causation: The analysis identifies patterns but does not explain why customers spend more or less. The suggested marketing strategies would need to be tested against actual customer responses and business outcomes.

Despite these limitations, the activity demonstrates how unsupervised machine learning can organize customer data into interpretable groups and provide insights for potential business applications.
